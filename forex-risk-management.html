Risk management

Forex risk management for beginners

Risk management cannot remove uncertainty, but it can help explain how much capital is exposed and how losses can compound.

Decide what you can afford to risk

Only consider risking money you can afford to lose without affecting essential expenses, savings goals or debt obligations. Avoid borrowing to trade. A risk limit is a planning tool, not a guarantee that a loss will stay within that amount.

Understand position sizing

Position size connects the amount at risk to the distance between an entry price and a planned exit if the trade moves against you. A smaller stop distance does not automatically mean a safer trade; the position size and market conditions matter too. Use a calculator only after checking pip value, contract size and account currency.

Stop orders are not guarantees

A stop order can help define an exit level, but in fast or illiquid markets the execution price can differ from the trigger price. Gaps, slippage and platform conditions can cause a larger loss than planned.

Leverage and margin

Leverage allows a trader to control a larger position relative to the funds deposited. It magnifies exposure and can cause losses to accumulate quickly. Margin requirements, close-out rules and negative-balance protections vary by provider and jurisdiction.

Understand drawdowns

A drawdown is a decline from a previous account peak. Losses and recoveries are not symmetrical: after a 50% loss, a 100% gain is needed just to return to the starting value. Avoid increasing risk simply to recover a previous loss.

Keep a trading journal

Record the reason for each decision, planned risk, actual costs, execution, outcome and whether you followed your rules. Review patterns over a meaningful sample rather than judging a method from one or two outcomes.

Continue learning

Review how currency pairs work and try the position size calculator with manually verified inputs.
