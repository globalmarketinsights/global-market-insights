Forex leverage and margin explained

Understand leverage, required margin, margin close-out and why a small market move can have a large effect on account equity.

← Back to all guides

What is leverage?

Leverage lets a trader control a position whose notional value is larger than the funds set aside as margin. For example, leverage of 1:20 means a position may be twenty times the margin amount under a simplified calculation. It does not reduce the economic risk of the position.

What is margin?

Margin is collateral required to open or maintain a leveraged position. A simplified initial-margin estimate is position notional value divided by leverage. Actual requirements depend on the instrument, provider, jurisdiction, account and market conditions.

Why leverage magnifies losses

Profit and loss are driven by the size of the underlying position, not only by the margin deposited. A relatively small adverse move can therefore consume a large share of the account's equity. Costs and slippage can add to the loss.

Margin level and close-out

Providers may monitor equity relative to used margin. If the account falls below specified thresholds, the provider may issue a margin warning or close positions. Exact thresholds, sequence and protections vary. Read the customer agreement and regulator information before opening an account.

Safer questions to ask

  • What leverage limit applies to this product and jurisdiction?
  • How is margin calculated and when can requirements change?
  • What is the provider's margin close-out policy?
  • Can losses exceed the funds in the account?
  • What happens during market gaps or extreme volatility?

Keep the distinction clear

Lower margin requirements do not make a trade safer. Understand the full position size, maximum plausible loss and provider rules before considering leveraged products. The risk-management guide explains position sizing and drawdowns.

Remember: This guide is for general education, not personal financial advice. Research independently and understand the risks before making financial decisions.
