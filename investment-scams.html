Investor safety

How to spot investment scams

Use these checks before sending money, sharing documents or connecting a wallet to an unfamiliar financial service.

Promises that sound too good to be true

Be cautious of guaranteed profits, fixed high returns, claims of no risk, secret trading systems and offers that promise to recover losses. Real markets involve uncertainty, and no legitimate trader can guarantee investment profits.

Pressure and urgency

Scammers may claim that an offer expires in minutes, that a special allocation is almost full or that you must deposit immediately. Slow down. Verify claims independently and do not let a countdown or personal pressure replace due diligence.

Fake platforms and testimonials

Professional-looking websites, dashboards and screenshots can be fabricated. A displayed account balance does not prove that funds exist or can be withdrawn. Search for independent information, verify the company's legal identity and test claims through official channels.

Impersonation and recovery scams

Fraudsters may impersonate exchanges, regulators, public figures, customer support or people you know. After an initial loss, another scammer may promise to recover the funds for an upfront fee. Be wary of unsolicited contact and never pay a stranger to unlock supposed profits.

Verify a provider

  • Find the regulator's official website yourself and search its register.
  • Check the exact legal entity, licence scope and country coverage.
  • Use contact details published by the regulator or provider, not details in a message.
  • Read withdrawal rules, fees and complaints information before depositing.
  • Check whether the provider is authorised to serve residents of your country.

Protect your accounts and wallet

Never share passwords, one-time codes, private keys or recovery phrases. Use unique passwords and multi-factor authentication where available. Do not install remote-access software at the request of an unsolicited caller.

If you think you have been scammed

Stop sending money, preserve messages and transaction records, contact your bank or service provider through official channels, and report the incident to the relevant authorities in your country. Be suspicious of anyone who contacts you promising guaranteed recovery.
