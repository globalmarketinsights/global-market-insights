Bitcoin basics: how the network works

Learn the core ideas behind Bitcoin before deciding whether to use, hold or study it.

What is Bitcoin?

Bitcoin is a digital asset and payment network. It allows participants to send bitcoin between addresses without relying on one central bank or company to maintain the transaction ledger. The network uses open-source software and a distributed group of computers to validate and record transactions.

What is the blockchain?

Bitcoin transactions are collected into blocks. Each block refers to the previous block, creating a chronological chain. Network participants independently verify that transactions follow the protocol's rules, including that coins are not spent twice.

What is mining?

Mining is the process by which specialised computers compete to add new blocks using proof-of-work. Miners expend computing resources, and the protocol adjusts the difficulty of the work. Mining helps order transactions and makes rewriting the history increasingly costly, but it does not make the system risk-free.

Bitcoin supply

Bitcoin's protocol is designed around a maximum supply of 21 million bitcoin. New bitcoin is issued through block rewards, which reduce over time according to the protocol. A supply limit does not guarantee that the market price will rise or that bitcoin will retain value.

How transactions work

A user creates a transaction using a wallet. The transaction is broadcast to the network, checked against protocol rules and eventually included in a block. Fees can vary with network demand. Confirmations provide increasing assurance that a transaction is part of the accepted chain.

Risks to understand

  • Market prices can rise or fall sharply.
  • Sending to the wrong address may be irreversible.
  • Lost private keys can mean permanent loss of access.
  • Exchanges and wallet software can fail or be compromised.
  • Rules and tax treatment differ between countries.
