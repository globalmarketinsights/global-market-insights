How forex currency pairs work

A practical explanation of currency quotes and the trading terms used to describe small price movements and costs.

Base and quote currencies

A forex pair contains two currencies. In EUR/USD, EUR is the base currency and USD is the quote currency. A price of 1.0800 means one euro is quoted at 1.08 US dollars. The first currency is always the base currency in the pair notation.

Bid and ask prices

The bid is the price at which a provider is willing to buy the base currency, while the ask is the price at which it is willing to sell it. The ask is usually higher than the bid. The difference is the spread, which is one of the costs of trading.

What is a pip?

A pip is a commonly used unit for expressing a small movement in an exchange rate. For many pairs it is the fourth decimal place, while pairs involving the Japanese yen are commonly quoted using the second decimal place. Some platforms also display fractional pip increments.

Major, minor and exotic pairs

Market convention often describes the most actively traded pairs involving the US dollar as major pairs. Cross-currency pairs that do not include the US dollar are often called minor pairs. Exotic pairs typically combine a major currency with a currency from a smaller or emerging market. Definitions can vary between providers.

Why exchange rates move

Exchange rates can respond to interest-rate expectations, inflation, economic releases, central-bank decisions, political developments, capital flows and changes in risk appetite. A single data point rarely explains every price movement.

Costs and risks

Compare spreads, commissions, overnight financing, conversion charges and withdrawal terms. Leverage can magnify both gains and losses. A quoted price does not guarantee the price at which a trade will execute.
