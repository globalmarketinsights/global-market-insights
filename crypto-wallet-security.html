Crypto safety

Crypto wallet security: protect your access

Learn what a wallet controls, how recovery phrases work and how to reduce common security mistakes.

What does a crypto wallet do?

A wallet manages the cryptographic keys used to authorise transactions. It does not literally store coins like a physical purse. Assets are recorded on the relevant blockchain, while the keys provide the ability to move them.

Custodial and non-custodial wallets

With a custodial service, a provider controls the keys on a customer's behalf. This can be convenient, but users depend on the provider's security, solvency, account access and withdrawal policies. With a non-custodial wallet, the user controls the keys and is responsible for backing them up and keeping them safe.

Hot and cold wallets

Hot wallets are connected to internet-enabled devices and are convenient for regular use. Cold storage keeps keys offline or more isolated from internet-connected systems. Each approach has trade-offs involving convenience, security, recovery and operational mistakes.

Protect your recovery phrase

  • Never share a recovery phrase or private key with anyone.
  • Legitimate support staff should not ask you to reveal it.
  • Do not type it into websites reached through unsolicited messages.
  • Keep backups private and protected from theft, fire and water damage.
  • Be cautious of screenshots, cloud notes and untrusted backup apps.

Before sending a transaction

Check the network, destination address, asset and amount. Be alert to address-substitution malware and fake support accounts. For a new recipient or unfamiliar service, consider a small test transaction first, while remembering that extra transactions can incur extra fees.

Token approvals and phishing

Some blockchain applications ask users to approve token spending. Review what a permission allows and avoid signing transactions you do not understand. Fake airdrops, cloned websites, malicious browser extensions and urgent direct messages are common ways scammers try to obtain access.
