Forex education

Learn how forex works

A clear introduction to the foreign-exchange market and the terminology beginners often encounter.

What is forex?

Foreign exchange, commonly called forex or FX, is the market where one currency is exchanged for another. Exchange rates can move in response to interest rates, inflation, economic data, central-bank decisions, political events and market expectations.

Understanding currency pairs

Forex is quoted in pairs, such as EUR/USD. The first currency is the base currency and the second is the quote currency. A quote indicates how much of the quote currency is needed to buy one unit of the base currency.

If EUR/USD is quoted at 1.0800, the simplified interpretation is that one euro is worth 1.08 US dollars at that quoted rate. Actual execution prices can differ due to spreads and fees.

Pips, spreads and leverage

  • Pip: A commonly used unit for measuring a small change in an exchange rate. The convention can vary by currency pair.
  • Spread: The difference between the bid and ask prices. It is one of the costs of trading.
  • Leverage: Allows exposure larger than the cash deposited. It magnifies losses as well as gains and can lead to rapid losses.

Why risk management matters

Forex trading is risky, especially when leverage is involved. Learn how position sizing, stop orders, trading costs and drawdowns work before considering any trade. A stop order may not always execute at the requested price during fast or illiquid markets.

Questions to ask before choosing a broker

  • Is the firm properly authorised in the jurisdiction where it operates?
  • Who holds client funds, and what protections apply?
  • What are the full spreads, commissions, swap charges and withdrawal conditions?
  • Can you verify the regulator's register independently?
  • Are you being pressured to deposit quickly or promised guaranteed returns?
