Forex spreads, commissions and swaps

Learn about the common costs of a forex position, including spreads, commissions, overnight financing and execution differences.

← Back to all guides

The spread

The spread is the difference between the bid price (where a market participant may sell) and the ask price (where a market participant may buy). A wider spread generally means a higher immediate trading cost. Spreads can change with liquidity, volatility, time of day and provider.

Commissions

Some providers charge a separate commission, often based on trade size or transaction value. Compare the commission together with the spread rather than treating either figure in isolation.

Swap or overnight financing

Positions held beyond a provider's daily cut-off may incur or receive a financing adjustment. The amount can depend on the currency pair, position direction, interest-rate conditions and broker methodology. Special calendar-day adjustments may apply.

Slippage and execution

Slippage is the difference between an expected execution price and the price actually obtained. Fast markets, gaps, low liquidity and order type can affect execution. A stop order does not always guarantee the requested exit price.

How to compare total costs

  • Check typical and variable spreads, not just a headline minimum.
  • Read the commission schedule and minimum charges.
  • Check overnight financing for both long and short positions.
  • Understand deposit, withdrawal, conversion and inactivity fees.
  • Read the execution policy and order terms.
  • Use the same trade size, pair and holding period when comparing providers.

Practise with estimates

Use the free tools centre to explore position sizing and risk. A calculator cannot reproduce every provider's pricing or execution conditions, so verify the actual contract specifications.
