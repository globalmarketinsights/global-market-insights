Stablecoins explained

Understand stablecoins, common designs, reserve questions and the risks that remain even when a token aims to track a currency.

← Back to all guides

What is a stablecoin?

A stablecoin is a digital token designed to maintain a relatively stable value, often by referencing a currency such as the US dollar. People may use stablecoins to transfer value between platforms or interact with blockchain applications.

A target value is a design goal, not a guarantee. A token can trade above or below its intended reference value.

Common stablecoin designs

  • Fiat-reserve models: The issuer says tokens are backed by cash, bank deposits or short-term assets. Users need clear information about reserves, custody, audits or attestations and redemption rights.
  • Crypto-collateralised models: These may lock other crypto assets as collateral. Price volatility can trigger liquidations, and the system depends on smart contracts and risk parameters.
  • Algorithmic or incentive-based models: These attempt to manage supply or demand through mechanisms and market incentives. They may be especially vulnerable when confidence falls.

Questions to investigate

  1. Who issues the token and in which jurisdiction?
  2. What assets, if any, support the token?
  3. Can ordinary holders redeem it, and under what conditions?
  4. Who controls the smart contracts, reserve accounts or freezing functions?
  5. What independent evidence supports claims about backing?
  6. Which blockchain and network fees apply?

Risks to remember

Stablecoins can face reserve, banking, issuer, regulatory, technology, liquidity and de-pegging risks. A token that tracks a foreign currency also exposes users to changes in that currency's value relative to their own local currency.

Do not assume that a stablecoin is a bank deposit, government-guaranteed money or risk-free savings. Read the issuer's current terms and independent disclosures.

Remember: This guide is for general education, not personal financial advice. Research independently and understand the risks before making financial decisions.
